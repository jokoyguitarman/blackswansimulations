About Black Swan Simulations

Built by someone who walked the ground.

Black Swan Simulations was founded by Kenneth Yeo, a terrorism and insurgency researcher with eight years of work on political violence in Southeast Asia — including field research inside conflict zones in Mindanao and Patani. The product exists because the crises he studied never behaved like the exercises meant to prepare people for them.

What we do

Crisis simulated. Readiness proven.

Black Swan Simulations runs live, AI-scored crisis exercises built on your organisation’s own risks, plans and operating procedures. Your people make real decisions under time pressure, every call is timed and graded, and you leave with measured evidence of where the response breaks.

01

Scenarios built from your real exposure

Our risk consultants turn your crisis plan, risk register and standard operating procedures into a bespoke, fictional scenario that reflects your sector, sites, stakeholders and decision thresholds.

02

Live simulations that push back

The situation moves on a clock and reacts to what your team does — or fails to do. A simulated feed, inbox and news cycle carries on regardless, and misinformation travels faster than your approval chain.

03

Evidence you can hand to a board

Decision latency, coordination, robustness and impact — scored per team and per participant, assembled into an after-action review generated from the exercise rather than reconstructed from memory.

The full engagement is described on the simulations page. This page is about why it exists.

The field record

Eight years studying how crises actually unfold

Not from a desk. Kenneth’s research on terrorism and insurgency in Southeast Asia is grounded in regular field research inside active conflict zones.

In his career as a researcher, Kenneth regularly conducted field research in conflict zones such as Mindanao in the southern Philippines and Patani in southern Thailand — interviewing the people living through protracted insurgencies, mapping how violence, rumour and response interact on the ground.

That work produced peer-reviewed research, policy briefs and a running body of analysis on suicide bombings, militant networks, maritime threats and online radicalisation — and it has been briefed to the international agencies that deal with these threats directly.

The method

Trained to find the blindspot, not to run the software

Alongside his research career, Kenneth is an active volunteer heavily involved in youth leadership development. Years of designing workshops and running focus group discussions built the facilitation craft the exercises depend on: drawing out what an organisation genuinely believes about itself, and testing it.

Workshop design

Exercises structured so that pressure lands where the plan is weakest — not where the agenda says the discussion should go.

Focus group discussion

The craft of getting a room to say the true thing rather than the safe thing — which is where organisational blindspots first surface.

From blindspot to practical step

Facilitation that does not stop at the finding: every uncovered gap is converted into a concrete, prioritised change your team can actually make.

The same skills now shape every Black Swan after-action review
