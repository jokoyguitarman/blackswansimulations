01 · The name

Before it emerges

Where it comes from

Prophylaxis is the quiet move in chess: a small, unspectacular move that prevents the opponent's massive attack before it can be built. The same word was adopted by medicine for prevention before onset, and by the brand names of vaccines. Prophyion keeps the essential of that word and builds a new name from it.

The thing that does it

Prophy = “before emerging”. Prophyion is the act, and the actor, of getting there first.

How to say it, how to write it

Pro · Fai · Yeon

The middle syllable is the Greek letter Φ (phi, “fai”) — concept A below plays on it; the selected mark tells the chess side of the story instead.

  • DoProphyion — capital P, one word, in running text and titles.
  • DoPROPHYION — only inside uppercase eyebrow labels and kickers.
  • DoUse the same name for the company and the platform.
  • Don't“Prophyion Simulations”, “Prophyion Sim”, or any suffix. The name stands alone.
  • Don't“ProPhyion”, “prophyion”, “Prophyon”, “Prophion”.
  • Don'tTranslate or hyphenate it (“Pro-phyion”) outside of pronunciation guides.
02 · Logo concepts

Ten marks, one wordmark

Every concept shares the same wordmark (Inter Bold, tracked −2.2%) and the same two-colour logic: navy for the structure, amber for the single decisive element. Each is drawn on a 64-unit grid and ships as SVG in light, dark, mono and tile forms. Concept J (Promotion · cut) is the selected mark and is wired into the product; A–I are kept here as the record of the exploration, and swapping is a file copy (see Assets).

A

Phi

Pro · Φ · ion

The disc is the event, whole and about to emerge. The amber bar is the prophylactic move: placed through it, before it, extending past it on both sides. Together they form the Greek Φ — the “fai” in Pro-fai-yeon — so the mark spells the middle of the name.

Reads as a single confident glyph at favicon size; the two-tone survives greyscale.

02b · Chess family

Pawn and rook, six ways

Variations on the chess origin: where the amber sits on the pawn, the rook as the giant of the set, the two together, and the pawn promoting into the rook. All share the same base heights so they can be mixed across touchpoints (e.g. rook as the app tile, Promotion in the wordmark lockup).

E

Rook

The giant · amber base

Battlements, a wide column, a heavy two-step base. The rook holds a file and closes a rank; it is the piece prophylaxis is usually protecting, and the one that looks most like a fortress. The amber base is the square it holds.

Reads better than the pawn at 16 px — the crenellations are unmistakable. Sober enough for government and defence audiences.

03 · Logo usage

Construction, clear space, minimum size

  • 1
    The crown — three merlons, 32 units wide on a 64 grid, on a solid bar. Always the “primary” colour (navy on light, cream on dark).
  • 2
    The cutout — the pawn at 60% scale, centred in the column with its head in the crown. It is a hole, not a fill: whatever is behind the mark shows through. Never fill it, never outline it.
  • 3
    The base — two steps; the lower step is the only amber element in the mark. In mono versions it takes the primary colour like everything else.

Clear space

One quarter of the mark's height on all sides (the dashed outer box). Nothing else, including the wordmark, enters it — the lockups already respect it.

Minimum size

Cutout legible from 24 px / 8 mm. The silhouette alone holds to 16 px; at that size use the plain rook (E) — the shipped favicon-16.png already does. Horizontal lockup: 120 px / 30 mm wide.

Which file

Light backgrounds → mark.svg. Navy or photo → mark-on-dark.svg. App icons, avatars, favicons → tile.svg. One-colour print → mark-mono.svg.

Don't rotate or tilt the mark.
Don't stretch or condense it.
Don't recolour outside the palette.
Don't put the light version on navy — use on-dark.
Don't fill the pawn cutout — not even amber. The pawn is negative space; the filled version (I) is a separate concept, not a variant.
Don't add the old white disc behind it. The tile replaces that job.
Don't add drop shadows, glows or outlines.
Prophyion Simulations
Don't append “Simulations” or any descriptor to the name.
Prophyion
Don't re-typeset the wordmark. Always place the SVG.

06 · Voice

Calm, exact, ahead of it

Lines already in use

  • “Crisis simulated. Readiness proven.” — primary tagline, title tags, OG image.
  • “Rehearse the crisis before it arrives.” — trailer close, secondary line.
  • “Leave with measured evidence of where your response breaks.” — meta description.
  • “Unified Simulation Environment” — product descriptor under the name in the app and footer.

Sounds like

An incident commander reading the log back to the room. Short declaratives, concrete nouns, numbers where numbers exist. The name's premise is “before”, so copy talks about what was prevented and what was measured, not about how advanced the technology is.

Avoid

  • No“Revolutionary”, “next-gen”, “AI-powered” as adjectives.
  • NoExclamation marks. Fear-selling.
  • NoSwan metaphors. The old name is retired; “black swan event” stays only where it is a term of art inside a scenario.
