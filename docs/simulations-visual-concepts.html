Visual concepts for /simulations

1 · Product proof 2 · Live replay 3 · Try a decision 4 · Imagery

Three ways to make the page prove the product exists rather than describe it. They differ in kind, not styling: one shows the real interface, one shows an exercise unfolding, one lets the visitor feel the scoring. Each is written to drop in below the hero.

Concept 1

Product proof

Inside an exercise

This is what your team will be looking at

Not slides. A live operating picture, a comms environment that fights back, and a scored record of every decision.

What it proves

The product is real and substantial. Highest credibility of the three, because nothing persuades a corporate buyer like seeing the actual interface.

What it needs from you

Four real screenshots, ideally from a populated session. The frames above are schematic stand-ins so you can judge the treatment.

Trade-off

Static. Shows the surface but not the tempo, and screenshots date as the UI changes.

What it proves

Tempo and consequence, which no screenshot conveys. Shows that the situation moves on a clock and that a slow team makes it worse.

What it needs from you

Nothing. It is pure CSS and adds roughly 2KB. Works today and never goes stale as the product UI changes.

Trade-off

It is a representation, not the product, so it carries less evidential weight than a real capture. Must stay labelled as illustrative.

Concept 3

Try a decision

Thirty seconds, one decision

See how your call would be scored

What happens next

In a real exercise this happens fifteen to twenty times, across four teams at once.

What it proves

Makes the abstract scoring concrete by grading the visitor. The latency figure is real: it measures how long they actually took to choose.

What it needs from you

A sign-off on the inject wording and the three scores, so the judgement reflects how your consultants would actually grade it.

Trade-off

Highest engagement, but asks the visitor to do something. Some will scroll past, so it should not be the only proof on the page.

Concept 4

Scenario imagery

Stylised illustration rather than photorealism, following the same reasoning Conducttr uses: painted imagery reads as a depiction, which suits a company whose entire promise is that the scenarios are fictional. Photoreal crisis footage would either look like stock photography or like real news, and neither helps you.

Treatment A · as card headers on the exercise types

Type 01

Multi-agency coordination

Tests the handoffs between your own functions and the external agencies you would actually depend on. Each team sees a different slice of the truth.

Type 02

Site and public safety response

For organisations responsible for physical sites and the people in them. A live map with a crowd simulation underneath that reacts to every decision.

Type 03

Reputational and social crisis

For communications, legal and leadership. A simulated feed and news cycle turns on your organisation while misinformation outruns your approval chain.

Treatment B · as a full-bleed band with the copy over it

What it proves

That the exercises are about recognisable, serious situations. Gives the page emotional weight the card grids cannot, and makes each exercise type instantly legible.

Weight

119KB for all three as WebP, down from 6.4MB as generated PNGs. Roughly quadruples the page weight but keeps it well under 200KB total.

Watch out for

Deliberately no gore, no destruction and no identifiable places or faces. Worth your sign-off that the tone is right for a government audience.

My recommendation

Ship 4 and 2 first: the imagery gives the page immediate weight and needs nothing from you, and the replay gives it tempo. Add 3 just above the enquiry form, where a visitor who is already interested will engage with it. Add 1 the moment you have real screenshots, because a real interface will always outrank a representation of one.

On the imagery, treatment A is the safer choice: three card headers that make each exercise type instantly legible without dominating the page. Treatment B has more impact but only works once, so it should be reserved for whichever exercise type you sell most.
