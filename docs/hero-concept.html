Illustrative interface views

Fictional, non-operational scenarios only — for government, defence, emergency services, critical infrastructure, corporate crisis and communications teams.

What changed and why

Composition

Three surfaces fanned and overlapping, bleeding past the right edge of the viewport so the frame feels too small for what is going on. The copy column is narrower than the visual, which is the opposite of the usual arrangement and is what stops it reading as a template. Hover the stack and it separates.

Built as interface, not art

Each card is real markup: an evacuating crowd drifting toward an exit, a disputed post climbing, an inbox filling with things that each want a different answer. It stays sharp at any resolution, weighs about 3KB, and the frames accept real screenshots later without redesigning anything.

The story it tells

Three things happening at once, none of which can wait, is the actual product. One panel could only ever show one. The counters move so a visitor who pauses sees the situation getting worse while they read.

On screens below 1024px the two back cards are hidden and the public feed card goes full width, so mobile stays fast and legible rather than showing a squashed fan.
